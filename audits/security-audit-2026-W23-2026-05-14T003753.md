## Scope

This audit focused on business-flow abuse rather than generic auth or infrastructure risk. The review specifically looked for ways a player could:

- move a building between companies below the intended valuation floor,
- borrow against collateral in one city or currency and settle from another without the right FX path,
- route debt repayment or liquidation into the wrong account or wrong currency,
- abuse foreclosure, default, or collateral transitions to strand lender exposure,
- exploit non-reserved offers or editable collateral to create unfair economic outcomes.

## Summary

This focused audit found four confirmed open business-logic issues and two partial-control gaps in the building, collateral, and lending flows.

The two strongest confirmed exploit paths are:

1. the building-sale floor only constrains the public asking price, not the accepted offer price, so a colluding seller and buyer can still settle a transfer at an arbitrarily low amount, and
2. several loan disbursement and repayment paths still use company balances without a currency filter, which breaks the multi-currency invariant the rest of the economy now relies on.

At the same time, several important abuse paths are already correctly mitigated: lot purchases require destination-city currency funding, direct bank-account transfers are same-currency only, company-context forex requires explicit matching-currency source and destination accounts, manual demolition is blocked while collateral is live, and forced-sale collateral settlement is explicitly converted between building currency and loan currency.

## Findings

### 1) Accepted Building Offers Can Bypass the Minimum Sale Floor

- **Severity:** Medium
- **Affected:** `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.RealEstate.cs`, `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.BuildingMarket.cs`, `projects/Api.Tests/BuildingSecondaryMarketTests.cs`
- **Description:** `setBuildingForSale` rejects an asking price below `BuildingMarketValuation.MinimumSalePrice`, but `makeOfferOnBuilding` only requires `offeredPrice > 0` and `acceptBuildingOffer` settles whatever pending offer is accepted. There is no backend revalidation that the accepted offer is at or above the minimum sale floor. Existing tests already demonstrate below-ask negotiation is allowed (`MakeOfferOnBuilding_CreatesOffer_WhenValid` offers `900_000` against a `1_000_000` ask).
- **Impact:** A colluding seller and buyer can legally list a building at a compliant public price and then transfer it privately for a nominal amount. That defeats the anti-arbitrage purpose of the sale floor and enables cheap asset shuffling between companies or players.
- **Abuse example:** list a building at the minimum allowed price, submit a token private offer from a friendly company, accept it, and move the asset below the intended floor anyway.
- **OWASP:** API6 Unrestricted Access to Sensitive Business Flows.
- **Recommendation:** Revalidate accepted price in `acceptBuildingOffer` against a server-side floor. If under-ask negotiation is still intended, the accepted offer should still not be allowed below the minimum market floor.
- **Status:** Open

### 2) Defaulted Collateral Can Be Reacquired Cheaply While Leaving Residual Debt Unsecured

- **Severity:** Medium
- **Affected:** `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.BuildingMarket.cs`, `projects/Api.Tests/BuildingSecondaryMarketTests.cs`
- **Description:** defaulted loans are intentionally allowed to list the collateral building for sale so the borrower can resolve the debt. `acceptBuildingOffer` settles debt from sale proceeds first, but if the sale proceeds are lower than the remaining principal it still clears `CollateralBuildingId` and leaves the loan in `Defaulted` state with residual principal outstanding.
- **Impact:** When combined with Finding 1, a borrower can default, list the collateral, sell it to a friendly company for a nominal amount, release the collateral from the loan, and leave the lender holding an unsecured defaulted balance with little or no recovery.
- **Abuse example:** borrower pledges a building, intentionally defaults, and transfers the building to an allied company for a tiny accepted offer so the lender receives only a fraction of the real collateral value.
- **OWASP:** API6 Unrestricted Access to Sensitive Business Flows.
- **Recommendation:** Forced-sale collateral transfers should enforce a lender-protective settlement floor. At minimum, a defaulted-collateral sale should not be able to clear the lien unless the enforced minimum sale proceeds satisfy the expected recovery rule.
- **Status:** Open

### 3) Loan Origination and Repayment Still Contain Currency-Blind Company Account Paths

- **Severity:** Medium
- **Affected:** `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.Lending.cs`, `projects/Api/Engine/Phases/LoanRepaymentPhase.cs`, `projects/Api/Utilities/CompanyBankingService.cs`
- **Description:** the loan itself is denominated in the lending bank's city currency, but several debit and credit paths do not enforce that currency:
  - `acceptLoan` checks `GetTotalBalance(lenderAccounts)` and stages `TryDebit(lenderAccounts, input.PrincipalAmount)` without a currency filter.
  - `LoanRepaymentPhase` credits the lender with `TryCredit(context.GetCompanyBankAccounts(lender.Id), totalPayment, null, out ...)`, which can pick any preferred company account regardless of loan currency.
  - `repayLoanDebt` falls back to currency-blind debit and credit if the configured repayment account is missing.
- **Impact:** A bank in one city can fund or receive loan cash using nominal balances from a different city currency with no FX conversion. That creates or destroys value across currencies depending on account ordering and balance distribution.
- **Abuse example:** a lender bank in Prague can originate a CZK loan while most of the lender company's available cash sits in EUR or PLN accounts; the current code will still debit the lender company's aggregate balances as if nominal units were interchangeable.
- **OWASP:** API6 Unrestricted Access to Sensitive Business Flows.
- **Recommendation:** Every debit and credit in the lending lifecycle must be explicitly scoped to the loan currency or bank city currency. Add mixed-currency lender tests for origination, scheduled repayment, manual debt repayment, and default recovery.
- **Status:** Open

### 4) Defaulted Loans Do Not Protect the Scheduled Repayment Account From Closure

- **Severity:** Medium
- **Affected:** `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.BuildingBankAccount.Accounts.cs`, `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.Lending.cs`
- **Description:** `closeCompanyBankAccount` blocks account closure only when the account is the repayment account for an `Active` or `Overdue` loan. `Defaulted` loans are not included. If the repayment account is then closed, `repayLoanDebt` falls back to `GetTotalBalance` and `TryDebit(..., payoffAmount)` without a currency filter.
- **Impact:** After default, a borrower can zero and close the designated repayment account and then repay the debt from arbitrary company balances across currencies, bypassing the original same-currency settlement rule.
- **Abuse example:** default a EUR loan, move the EUR cash out of the designated repayment account, close that account, then call `repayLoanDebt` while holding enough nominal value only in CZK or PLN accounts.
- **OWASP:** API6 Unrestricted Access to Sensitive Business Flows.
- **Recommendation:** block repayment-account closure for any unpaid loan status, including `Defaulted`, and reject manual debt repayment when the expected loan-currency settlement account is missing instead of silently falling back to aggregate company balances.
- **Status:** Open

### 5) Pledged Buildings Remain Editable After Loan Origination

- **Severity:** Low
- **Affected:** `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.BuildingConfiguration.cs`, `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.UnitUpgrade.cs`, `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.RealEstate.cs`
- **Description:** collateral locks are enforced for sale, transfer, and demolition, but `storeBuildingConfiguration` does not perform any collateral-lock check before applying due plans and queuing new ones. I found no equivalent collateral guard on the building-configuration path.
- **Impact:** A borrower can keep changing a pledged building's internal layout and unit plan after borrowing. Even without a direct refund path, this weakens the meaning of collateral and lets a borrower change the risk profile of the pledged asset without lender consent.
- **OWASP:** API6 Unrestricted Access to Sensitive Business Flows.
- **Recommendation:** either freeze value-reducing changes while a building is pledged, or re-appraise and revalidate collateral when such changes are requested.
- **Status:** Open

### 6) Pending Building Offers Are Not Escrowed or Globally Reserved

- **Severity:** Low
- **Affected:** `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.BuildingMarket.cs`
- **Description:** `makeOfferOnBuilding` checks the buyer company's current balance at offer time, but it does not reserve funds or track total pending exposure across buildings. A buyer can place multiple full-size pending offers on different buildings using the same money.
- **Impact:** This does not mint currency, but it enables market jamming and fake demand. Sellers can be induced to rely on offers that are not actually backed by reserved funds.
- **OWASP:** API4 Unrestricted Resource Consumption, API6 Unrestricted Access to Sensitive Business Flows.
- **Recommendation:** reserve funds, require an offer bond, or cap pending building-market exposure per company.
- **Status:** Open

### 7) Defaulted Residual Principal No Longer Counts Against New-Lending Capacity

- **Severity:** Low
- **Affected:** `projects/Api/Types/Mutation.Lending.cs`
- **Description:** available lending capacity is derived from active and overdue loan principal only. Defaulted loans with `RemainingPrincipal > 0` are excluded from the `outstandingPrincipal` calculation used for the 90% deposit-to-loan rule.
- **Impact:** A bank can reopen regulatory-style capacity immediately after loans default even though those losses have not been absorbed yet. The actual cash-balance check still limits some abuse, so this is weaker than Findings 1-4, but it still understates real outstanding credit risk.
- **OWASP:** API6 Unrestricted Access to Sensitive Business Flows.
- **Recommendation:** include all unpaid principal, including `Defaulted`, in the capacity calculation or make `LoanOffer.UsedCapacity` the authoritative constraint.
- **Status:** Partially mitigated

## Verified Mitigations

The following cheat paths were explicitly checked and appear to be correctly defended today:

| Scenario | Current status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Buy a lot in Prague without Prague currency funding | Mitigated | `PrepareLotPurchaseAsync` resolves a funding account in the lot city currency and returns `INSUFFICIENT_LOCAL_CURRENCY_FUNDS` when that balance is missing. |
| Assign an EUR account to a CZK building and spend from it | Mitigated | `assignBuildingBankAccount` enforces currency match; `BuildingBankAccountTests` and unit-upgrade FX tests expect `CURRENCY_MISMATCH`. |
| Transfer funds directly between own EUR and CZK accounts | Mitigated | `transferFunds` rejects cross-currency moves with `CURRENCY_MISMATCH`. |
| Use company-context forex with the wrong source or destination account currency | Mitigated | `executeForexSwap` requires both selected company accounts and validates `fromAccount.CurrencyCode == fromCode` and `toAccount.CurrencyCode == toCode`. |
| Destroy a live collateral building and keep the 80% refund | Mitigated | `destroyBuilding` checks `IsCollateralLockedAtCommitAsync` and rejects with `BUILDING_LOCKED_AS_COLLATERAL`. |
| Auto-list defaulted collateral in the bank currency instead of the property currency | Mitigated | `LoanRepaymentPhase` converts `CollateralAppraisedValue` into the building city currency before listing; `BuildingDestructionTests` cover the cross-currency case. |
| Settle forced-sale debt in the wrong currency after a defaulted collateral sale | Mitigated | `acceptBuildingOffer` explicitly converts sale proceeds into the loan currency and `BuildingSecondaryMarketTests` verify the FX ledger split. |
| Borrow into a settlement account whose currency does not match the lending bank city | Mitigated | `ResolveLoanSettlementAccountAsync` rejects the requested account with `CURRENCY_MISMATCH`. |

## Recommendations

1. Fix the accepted-offer floor first. It is the simplest confirmed asset-transfer arbitrage and directly undermines the existing sale-floor rule.
2. Make loan-currency invariants explicit everywhere in `Mutation.Lending` and `LoanRepaymentPhase`. No lending debit or credit should ever call `CompanyBankingService.TryDebit` or `TryCredit` without the loan currency.
3. Treat the repayment account as part of the unpaid-loan state machine. Defaulted debt must still block closure of the scheduled repayment account.
4. Freeze or revalidate collateralized buildings on configuration changes. A pledged building should not be freely mutable while it secures a live debt.
5. Add mixed-currency lender tests, defaulted-repayment-account closure tests, and below-floor accepted-offer tests before any further real-estate or lending work lands.

## Conclusion

The repository already contains several solid controls around local-currency property purchases, same-currency bank transfers, forced-sale FX settlement, and collateral demolition locks. The remaining problems are narrower but economically important: accepted offer prices can still bypass the intended building-sale floor, and the lending lifecycle still has multiple currency-blind debit and credit paths that are inconsistent with the bank-account model used elsewhere in the game. Those issues should be treated as the next business-logic hardening priority.
